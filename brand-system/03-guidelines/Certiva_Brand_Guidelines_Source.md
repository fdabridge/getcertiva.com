# Certiva Brand Guidelines

Version 1.0 | 5 October 2026 | Working production baseline

## 1. What Certiva is

Certiva is software purpose-built for the operating work of management-system certification bodies. It helps teams keep applications, audit planning, documents, signatures, review and decisions connected within a traceable case. The certification body's people retain technical judgment, impartiality, approval and certification responsibility.

The brand should make an experienced CB operator think: “They understand how the work moves between people and records.” It should not try to look like a regulator, an accreditation body, or an all-knowing AI replacement for auditors.

## 2. Brand character

- **Calm and exact:** show control through clear organization, not through fear or hype.
- **Operational:** describe what changes in a real workflow, not a pile of capabilities.
- **Peer-to-peer:** talk as someone who has handled certification work, while respecting each CB's procedures.
- **Evidence-first:** real UI, current workflows, explicit limitations, no invented customers or metrics.
- **Human-controlled:** AI can assist drafting and checks where implemented; people review and decide.

### One-line position

**Certification work, connected from application to decision.**

This is a positioning line for campaigns, not a permanent part of the logo. Use it only where the relevant workflow really supports the claim. Never imply every CB implementation is identical.

## 3. Logo

Use the supplied current-production files in `01-logos/`. The primary lockup is for white and pale surfaces; the reversed lockup is for Night, Surface and Forest. Use the compact icon for profile avatars and small controls. Clear space equals at least one quarter of the 44 px icon width at native scale. Minimum wordmark width is 120 px digital.

Do not redraw, stretch, recolor, add a glow, put it over unprotected footage, or attach a tagline. Never use another company's seal or accreditation mark next to the logo in a way that suggests ownership or endorsement.

**Source caveat:** the user's prior refined mark is in an iCloud folder that could not be read during this build. This package uses the website's current logo, verbatim. Swap only after recovering and comparing the approved master.

## 4. Color and contrast

Night `#060D08` and White `#FFFFFF` are the primary canvases. Forest `#1A4731` anchors the mark, light-background headings and buttons. Signal `#52C27A` is an accent on dark backgrounds. Use it for a thin status line, important action or highlighted term, not for a large glow wash. Ink `#111827` carries long text on white.

Accessible combinations: Ink on White, Forest on White, Mid on White, Signal on Night, Pale on Night, and White on Forest. Do not set small text in Signal on White or Leaf on White. Text and status meaning cannot rely on hue alone.

Full values and ratios: `02-color-and-type/Color_Specification.md`.

## 5. Type and hierarchy

Use Manrope for marketing and UI work, including the website. Prefer a two-level hierarchy: a direct heading, then explanatory body. Headlines are sentence case and usually left aligned. Use no more than two weights in one short asset. Monospace is reserved for actual field names, record IDs or code-like data, not every marketing claim.

Do not simulate authority with dense all-caps labels, tiny copy or faux technical dashboards. Follow the scale in `02-color-and-type/Typography.md`.

## 6. Layout and image treatment

Build on an 8 px spacing base. Protect at least 5% of every social canvas and 20 mm on A4. Keep one primary thought per screen or cover. Prefer a 60/40 or 55/45 editorial split when pairing an explanation with a real product screenshot. Let content align to a stable grid; avoid ornamental floating cards that obscure the workflow.

Use genuine, permission-cleared Certiva UI captures. Crop to the task being explained; leave enough context to understand what the user is doing. If a template uses an illustrative block, label it clearly and replace it before publishing. Never imply a mockup is a live product view. Blur or replace customer names, emails, documents, certificates, signatures and other sensitive data unless explicit publication rights exist.

Photography is optional. When used, choose licensed real people or work settings, not anonymous “AI robot” imagery. Keep licensing and release records with the asset.

## 7. Product-proof module

A good feature asset contains four parts:

1. **Operational trigger:** what makes the task necessary?
2. **One workflow:** where does the person start, what moves, and where does it stop?
3. **Visible proof:** a real screenshot, brief clip, or accurate trace of an existing workflow.
4. **Human outcome:** what becomes easier to verify, review or coordinate?

Example: “After application approval, the audit set is prepared with known case data. The planner reviews the files and downloads the pack.” This is better than “Revolutionary AI instantly creates everything.”

For electronic signing, show the actual signing area and the document status, not merely a generic pen icon. Never imply certificates require a signature if that is not the active workflow.

## 8. Voice and writing

- Lead with the reader's problem or handoff, not the entire feature list.
- Use specific nouns: application, audit plan, document set, reviewer, signature, decision.
- Explain the controlled result: records remain connected, known fields carry forward, the next person can see what is outstanding.
- State the role of people: the CB reviews, approves and owns certification decisions.
- Give one next action: see a relevant workflow, ask a question, or request a guided demo.

### Good / avoid

| Good | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| “The approved application feeds the prepared audit set.” | “Revolutionize every certification process instantly.” |
| “The reviewer sees the current case and outstanding items.” | “AI guarantees accreditation compliance.” |
| “Show us your handoff; we'll demonstrate that workflow.” | “Book a demo to discover 50 game-changing features.” |

No unverified statistics, audit-day claims, accreditation promises, or competitor insults. When a capability is not fully supported in production, say what is available now and what remains under development.

## 9. Social and launch

The default social asset is a concise point on Night or White, a restrained green cue, and one product-proof frame. The logo appears once. Use platform-specific safe areas and test at phone size. A launch post can tell the founder story, but it should return quickly to the buyer's operational problem. Do not inflate the company size or imply adoption by CBs without permission.

Content families: one workflow explained, operator insight, product proof, customer question, implementation clarity, and founder perspective. Details and editable SVGs are in `05-social/`.

## 10. Documents and email

Proposals and product briefs must name the actual legal seller separately from the Certiva brand where a commercial agreement requires it. They need version/date, recipient, scope, assumptions, data responsibilities, support and security commitments when relevant. No template itself is a certification decision or accreditation statement.

Email signatures are compact: sender, role, Certiva, one website link and one reply address. A postal address or phone number may be added only after the responsible entity and contact details are verified for that communication.

## 11. Pre-release check

- Is the logo current and undistorted?
- Is the screenshot a real, current, permission-cleared UI state?
- Is every claim supported by current product behavior?
- Does the asset explain one workflow and a human-controlled outcome?
- Are color contrast, type size, cropping and safe zones sound at the intended size?
- Are names, documents, signatures and personal data protected?
- Is the next action clear without being coercive?
- Are the brand name, domain and any legal entity references correct?
